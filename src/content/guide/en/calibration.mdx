A simulation is only as believable as its results. The match engine is therefore run on tens of
thousands of matches after every change, and the results are compared with real NHL averages. This
page shows those results. It does not show how the engine gets there - weights, thresholds and
internal formulas stay private, so that the game is won by hockey decisions, not by reverse
engineering.

<Callout type="note" title="Measurement">
  Engine version e60b4955, measured on 10 November 2026. Two evenly matched teams with default
  tactics: 80,000 matches. Every strength gap: 40,000 matches. The reference is the NHL 2024/25
  regular season (Hockey-Reference, see [Sources](../sources/)).
</Callout>

| Shot attempts | 58.1 | - |
| Goals by period | 1.00 / 1.00 / 0.99 | - |

<Callout type="tip" title="What the numbers say">
  An ordinary Ice Flow game looks like an ordinary NHL game: about three goals a team, just under
  thirty shots, a goalie stopping nine shots in ten and a power-play goal in roughly every fifth
  chance. Goals are spread evenly across the three periods.
</Callout>

## How games end

| | Ice Flow |
|---|---|
| Wins of the first of two equal teams | 50.1 % ± 0.35 |
| Level after 60 minutes | 16.8 % of games |
| Decided in overtime | 12.0 % of games |
| Decided in a shootout | 4.8 % of games |

Two equal teams win equally often within the statistical margin - the first check that nothing in the
engine favours one side.

## When one team is stronger

The stronger team should win more often, but not always. In these matches **every attribute of every
player on one team, goalies included, is higher by the given number of points** - on the same scale
the app shows - and everything else is equal.

<Callout type="note" title="Upsets happen">
  A team ten points better still loses about one game in five, and twenty points better about one in
  ten.
  A better team also wins by outshooting the opponent, not only by scoring more from the same shots.
</Callout>

## The same game at every level

A league of weaker players has to play like a league of stronger ones - otherwise lower leagues
would look different from top ones. Here every attribute of both teams is 40 points lower or higher.

| | Weaker league (-40) | Average league | Stronger league (+40) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equal teams, wins | 50.6 % | 50.1 % | 48.9 % |
| Goals per team | 3.09 | 3.09 | 3.05 |
| Shots on goal per team | 29.6 | 29.6 | 29.5 |
| Save percentage | 89.7 % | 89.7 % | 89.6 % |
| Power-play success | 20.1 % | 20.2 % | 19.9 % |
| A team 10 points better wins | 78.8 % | 78.6 % | 78.3 % |

The game plays the same way whatever the level of the league; only the gap between the two teams
decides who is favoured.

## What is not shown here

- the weights and formulas of the engine,
- the effect of individual tactics, player types and their combinations,
- how attributes are combined inside the engine.

These are left out on purpose. The aim of this page is to show that the results are realistic and
fair, not to give a recipe for gaming them.
