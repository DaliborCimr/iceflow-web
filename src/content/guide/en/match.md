## How a match is simulated

Every match is simulated on the server, second by second, and streamed to the app as it is played.
Play moves from one situation to the next - the puck is won, carried forward, a chance appears, the
goalie makes a save or play stops. Each of these transitions is a calibrated probability.

What shifts those probabilities is what is on the ice at that moment:

- the players on the ice and their attributes, energy and form,
- the tactic and risk level of each line,
- how well the players suit the tactic,
- the manpower situation - even strength, power play, penalty kill, empty net,
- the stakes of the game, where players' mentality comes into play.

The same match played under the same conditions gives the same result, so a game can be replayed
and checked. How the probabilities are calibrated against real hockey is described on the
Calibration page.

## Lines and units

You set four lines of three forwards and two defensemen, a starting and a backup goalie, two
power-play units and two penalty-kill units.

- **Ice time.** A preset divides playing time between the lines. Changes follow the players'
  energy: a tired player loses energy faster and plays worse.
- **Power play.** Two units of five: three forwards, a defenseman and a flex spot. The unit with
  the most energy goes on. Against two penalties the unit plays all five; in a 4 on 3 one skater sits
  out.
- **Penalty kill.** Two units of four - two forwards and two defensemen. A three-man kill is the two
  defensemen and the unit's first forward.
- **4 on 4 and 3 on 3.** When both sides are short, the line on the ice is cut down: two defensemen
  and two forwards, or two forwards and a defenseman.
- **Goalies.** The starter plays; the backup comes in if the starter is injured. A goalie's fatigue
  carries over from game to game.

## Tactics and risk

Each line has an attack tactic and a defense tactic. There are six of each:

| Attack | | Defense | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quick Strike | fast breakouts and an early attack | Puck Hunt | pressure on the puck carrier in every zone |
| Stretch Counter | long passes and immediate counterattacks | Strong-Side Lock | steering the puck to one side and closing the boards |
| Possession Cycle | sustained puck movement along the boards | Man Marking | tight coverage of individual opponents |
| Direct Pressure | dump-ins, forechecking and physical pressure | Layered Zone | compact layers protecting dangerous areas |
| Point & Traffic | shots from the blue line, screens and rebounds | Neutral Trap | closing the middle of the neutral zone |
| Slot Overload | quick rotations and short passes to the slot | Low Collapse | defending close to the net |

The tactics are based on real systems described in coaching materials and analyses (see Sources).

Every tactic suits some player types and does not suit a few; the tactic's description lists them.
Fit is measured against the average player, so no tactic is better than another on its own. A tactic
pays off when a line has more players it suits than usual.

The **risk level** goes from Lockdown through Control and Balanced to Press and All Out. The more a
line risks, the more it pushes forward and shoots - and the more chances it gives away.

## Preparation and scouting

Before a match you can scout the opponent once. Scouting shows the strength of its saved lines, a
report from up to five recent games and the stakes of the game for both teams, and it gives your
team a small preparation bonus that grows with your professional scouting staff. The opponent can
still change its lineup before kickoff.

## Match stakes

The stakes show how much the game means to each team - one bar for an ordinary league game, more in
a fight for the playoff or a better seed, all five in the playoff. They can differ between the two
teams. The higher the stakes, the more the players' mentality matters in tense moments.

## During the match

While the game is on you see who is on the ice, each player's energy, the events as they happen and
a running box score. You can change the lineup and tactics; a saved change plays from the moment it
takes effect. In the last five minutes, when behind, you can pull the goalie for an extra attacker.
Goals and selected chances are shown as animated highlights chosen to match the real event - the
manpower situation and the number of assists.
