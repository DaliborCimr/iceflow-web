Every match is played out on the server in real time and streamed to your phone. You are not just
watching a result - you can coach while the game is on.

## How a match is simulated

Play moves from one situation to the next: a team wins the puck, carries it forward, a chance
appears, the goalie makes a save or play stops. Each of these moments is decided by a **calibrated
probability**, and that probability is shifted by what is on the ice right then.

<Rules columns>
  <Rule icon="groups" title="Who is on the ice">
    The players' attributes, their energy and form, and how they suit the tactic.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="route" title="How they play">
    Each line's attack and defense tactic and its risk level.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="exposure" title="The manpower situation">
    Even strength, power play, penalty kill, 4 on 4, empty net.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="psychology" title="What is at stake">
    In important games, players' mentality matters more in tense moments.
  </Rule>
</Rules>

The same match under the same conditions gives the same result, so every game can be replayed and
checked. How the probabilities are tuned against real hockey is on the
[Calibration](../calibration/) page.

## When one team is stronger

In a match, a team's strength comes from its players' attributes. The chart shows how often a team wins when **every attribute of every player, goalies included, is higher
by the given number of points** than the opponent's, with everything else equal. Measured on 40,000
matches per gap.

<StrengthChart />

A better team is a clear favourite, but upsets stay part of the game: a team five points better wins
about two games in three, ten points better about four in five. More numbers are on the
[Calibration](../calibration/) page.

## Your lines

<Rules>
  <Rule icon="view_list" title="Four lines">
    Each is three forwards and two defensemen. A line's share of ice time decides how often it
    plays; changes follow the players' energy, because a tired player loses energy faster and plays
    worse.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="sports_hockey" title="Goalies">
    The starter begins the game. The backup can replace an injured starter, or you can switch
    goalies while coaching live. The change is shown in the match timeline. A goalie’s fatigue
    carries over from game to game.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="bolt" title="Power play: PP1 and PP2">
    Two units of five - three forwards, a defenseman and a flex spot. The unit with the most energy
    goes on. Against two penalties it plays all five; in a 4 on 3 one skater sits out.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="shield" title="Penalty kill: PK1 and PK2">
    Two units of four - two forwards and two defensemen. A three-man kill is the two defensemen and
    the unit's first forward.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="groups_2" title="4 on 4 and 3 on 3">
    When both sides are short, the line is cut down to two defensemen and two forwards, or two
    forwards and a defenseman.
  </Rule>
</Rules>

<Rules>
  <Rule icon="handshake" title="Chemistry">
    Skaters who play together get used to each other. Every pair of skaters at the club has its
    chemistry: it grows with the minutes they share on the ice in official games - quickly at
    first, more slowly as it gets high - and slowly fades when they do not play together. A line
    with better chemistry than the line it faces wins its battles for the puck a little more
    often; on special teams it counts half as much. A newcomer starts with no chemistry with
    anyone. Chemistry opens in your club's fifth season; until then, and for bots always, every
    pair sits at a middle value that changes nothing. The Team screen shows it under the line's
    values - the average of its skaters' pairs.
  </Rule>
</Rules>

<Callout type="note" title="Why units of five, not forward lines and defense pairs">
  In real hockey forward lines and defense pairs are put together and change separately. A team
  usually dresses four lines and three pairs ([Dummies](https://www.dummies.com/sports/hockey/hockey-substitutions-deciding-who-plays/)) - at most 18 skaters and two
  goalkeepers ([NHL 5.1](https://media.d3.nhle.com/image/private/t_document/prd/i9yumvyaojps5kixzaxz.pdf)) - so defensemen play more than forwards.

  Ice Flow builds the lineup from four units of five on purpose: a tactic belongs to the whole unit.
  The attack and defense tactics, the risk level and the ice-time share are set for five players at
  once, and how well a tactic suits a line is the sum of all five. On a phone one line is one set of
  choices. The cost is that the game dresses eight defensemen instead of six and defensemen get no
  ice time of their own apart from their forwards.

  In the match, though, a unit does not change as one block: players go to the bench by energy, so a
  unit's forwards and defensemen sometimes split up on the ice, even though they play together most
  of the time.
</Callout>

## Tactics

Each line has an **attack tactic** - how it plays with the puck - and a **defense tactic** - how it
plays without it. Each one is a real system described in coaching materials and analyses - the
sources are listed under the tables, and which player types suit each tactic is on
[Player types](../player-types/).

| Attack | What the line does |
|---|---|
| Quick Strike | Fast, supported breakouts and an attack before the defense sets up |
| Stretch Counter | Long passes to high forwards, straight into a counterattack |
| Possession Cycle | Keeps the puck moving along the boards and behind the net |
| Direct Pressure | Dumps the puck in and wins it back with forechecking and physical play |
| Point & Traffic | Shots from the blue line with screens, deflections and rebounds |
| Slot Overload | Quick rotations and short passes into the slot |

| Defense | What the line does |
|---|---|
| Puck Hunt | Pressures the puck carrier in every zone |
| Strong-Side Lock | Steers the puck to one side and closes the boards |
| Man Marking | Tight coverage of individual opponents |
| Layered Zone | Compact layers protecting the dangerous areas |
| Neutral Trap | Closes the middle of the neutral zone and forces dump-ins |
| Low Collapse | Defends close to the net and protects the slot |

<Callout type="tip" title="No tactic is best on its own">
  Every tactic suits some player types and does not suit a few - the description in the app lists
  them. Fit is measured against an average player, so a typical line is equal in every tactic. A
  tactic pays off when your line has more players it suits than usual.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note" title="Why power plays and penalty kills have no tactic">
  Tactics are balanced for full-strength play. They apply at 5 on 5 and 4 on 4; power plays,
  penalty kills, 3 on 3 and play without a goalie are played without a tactic, and the PP and PK
  units have no tactic to choose. Tactics for these situations are being researched; they will come
  once they are backed by sources and balanced.
</Callout>

## Risk and ice time

<Rules>
  <Rule icon="speed" title="Risk level">
    From Lockdown through Control and Balanced to Press and All Out. The more a line risks, the more
    it pushes forward and shoots - and the more chances it gives the opponent. A careful line keeps
    the puck and plays it safe.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="pie_chart" title="Ice-time presets">
    A preset divides playing time between your four lines. You do not pick which opponent line
    yours will face.
  </Rule>
</Rules>

## Before the match

<Screenshot name="preparation" alt="Match preparation: line ratings of both teams, risk levels and the scouting report" caption="Preparation: both teams' line ratings after scouting, risk per line and the report from recent games." />

<Rules>
  <Rule icon="search" title="Scouting the opponent">
    Once per match, for your next league or Elite Series game. Before scouting, the opponent’s
    line ratings are question marks. Scouting reveals an estimate of the lines it would dress,
    including automatic replacements for unavailable players, and a report from up to five recent
    games. The opponent may still change its lineup before kickoff.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="military_tech" title="Preparation bonus">
    Scouting also gives your team a small bonus for that match, which grows with your professional
    scouting staff.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="local_fire_department" title="Match extras">
    From your club's third season, once scouted, the preparation also offers VIP logistics for an away game and a dressing-room
    bonus, paid in Flow - see [optional expenses](../club/#optional-expenses).
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="signal_cellular_alt" title="Match stakes">
    The scale runs from one to four bars, with different stakes possible for each team. In the
    regular season it reflects the fight for the playoff, a top-two Elite Series place, home ice
    and a better seed. The top two matters more than the top four for a home quarterfinal.
    A league quarterfinal opener shows two bars; leading the series shows three, facing
    elimination four. A deciding third game and single-game knockout rounds show four.
    Higher stakes make mentality count more in tense moments.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="schedule" title="When the stakes are known">
    In the group stage, after the previous round is complete. In a single-game playoff round,
    as soon as the pairing is drawn; a league quarterfinal waits only for the preceding game of
    its own series, not the other series. The stakes appear in the scouting report after you scout.
  </Rule>
</Rules>

## During the match

<Screenshot name="live-match" alt="Live match: score, period clock and both teams' players on the ice with their energy" caption="Live: who is on the ice for both teams and the energy of your players." />

<Rules>
  <Rule icon="visibility" title="What you see">
    Who is on the ice for both teams and the energy of your own players, goals, penalties and
    injuries as they happen (an injured player gets a badge), the power-play time left and a running
    box score. The opponent's energy stays hidden. Elsewhere in the app a cyan dot on the menu
    tells you your match is live; it takes priority over the red dot.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="groups" title="What you can change">
    Lineup, tactics, risk, ice-time preset and goalie. A saved change plays from the moment it
    takes effect until the end. It works with the players dressed for the game and holds for this
    match only - your lineup for later games stays as it was.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="person_pin_circle" title="Being there">
    Watching your team's live match helps it by itself, with no change at all: joining during a
    period gives the team a small edge from that moment. Being there before it starts earns the
    whole of it - the warm-up earns the first period, the first intermission the second, the second
    intermission the third and the break before overtime the overtime. What a period has earned
    stays even if you leave.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="sports_hockey" title="Pulling the goalie">
    In the last five minutes of the third, when one to three goals behind, you can pull the goalie for
    an extra attacker. In real hockey a coach may pull the goalie at any time; Ice Flow limits it so that it
    cannot be abused - a losing team could leave its net empty to concede more on purpose, and a
    leading team could pull its goalie to mock the opponent.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="movie" title="Highlights">
    Goals and selected chances - missed shots included - are shown as animations chosen to match the
    real event: the manpower situation and the number of assists.
  </Rule>
</Rules>
