Every match is played out on the server in real time and streamed to your phone. You are not just
watching a result - you can coach while the game is on.

## How a match is simulated

Play moves from one situation to the next: a team wins the puck, carries it forward, a chance
appears, the goalie makes a save or play stops. Each of these moments is decided by a **calibrated
probability**, and that probability is shifted by what is on the ice right then.

<Rules columns>
  <Rule icon="groups" title="Who is on the ice">
    The players' attributes, their energy and form, and how they suit the tactic.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="route" title="How they play">
    Each line's attack and defense tactic and its risk level.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="exposure" title="The manpower situation">
    Even strength, power play, penalty kill, 4 on 4, empty net.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="psychology" title="What is at stake">
    In important games, players' mentality matters more in tense moments.
  </Rule>
</Rules>

The same match under the same conditions gives the same result, so every game can be replayed and
checked. How the probabilities are tuned against real hockey is on the
[Calibration](../calibration/) page.

## When one team is stronger

In a match, a team's strength comes from its players' attributes. The chart shows how often a team wins when **every attribute of every player, goalies included, is higher
by the given number of points** than the opponent's, with everything else equal. Measured on 40,000
matches per gap.

<StrengthChart />

A better team is a clear favourite, but upsets stay part of the game: a team five points better wins
about two games in three, ten points better about four in five. More numbers are on the
[Calibration](../calibration/) page.

## Your lines

<Rules>
  <Rule icon="sports_hockey" title="Four lines">
    Each is three forwards and two defensemen. A line's share of ice time decides how often it
    plays; changes follow the players' energy, because a tired player loses energy faster and plays
    worse.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="shield_person" title="Goalies">
    The starter plays the game; the backup comes in when the starter gets injured. A goalie's
    fatigue carries over from game to game.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="add_circle" title="Power play: PP1 and PP2">
    Two units of five - three forwards, a defenseman and a flex spot. The unit with the most energy
    goes on. Against two penalties it plays all five; in a 4 on 3 one skater sits out.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="do_not_disturb_on" title="Penalty kill: PK1 and PK2">
    Two units of four - two forwards and two defensemen. A three-man kill is the two defensemen and
    the unit's first forward.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="unfold_less" title="4 on 4 and 3 on 3">
    When both sides are short, the line is cut down to two defensemen and two forwards, or two
    forwards and a defenseman.
  </Rule>
</Rules>

## Tactics

Each line has an **attack tactic** - how it plays with the puck - and a **defense tactic** - how it
plays without it. They are based on real systems from coaching materials (see
[Sources](../sources/)).

| Attack | What the line does |
|---|---|
| Quick Strike | Fast, supported breakouts and an attack before the defense sets up |
| Stretch Counter | Long passes to high forwards, straight into a counterattack |
| Possession Cycle | Keeps the puck moving along the boards and behind the net |
| Direct Pressure | Dumps the puck in and wins it back with forechecking and physical play |
| Point & Traffic | Shots from the blue line with screens, deflections and rebounds |
| Slot Overload | Quick rotations and short passes into the slot |

| Defense | What the line does |
|---|---|
| Puck Hunt | Pressures the puck carrier in every zone |
| Strong-Side Lock | Steers the puck to one side and closes the boards |
| Man Marking | Tight coverage of individual opponents |
| Layered Zone | Compact layers protecting the dangerous areas |
| Neutral Trap | Closes the middle of the neutral zone and forces dump-ins |
| Low Collapse | Defends close to the net and protects the slot |

<Callout type="tip" title="No tactic is best on its own">
  Every tactic suits some player types and does not suit a few - the description in the app lists
  them. Fit is measured against an average player, so a typical line is equal in every tactic. A
  tactic pays off when your line has more players it suits than usual.
</Callout>

## Risk and ice time

<Rules>
  <Rule icon="speed" title="Risk level">
    From Lockdown through Control and Balanced to Press and All Out. The more a line risks, the more
    it pushes forward and shoots - and the more chances it gives the opponent. A careful line keeps
    the puck and plays it safe.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="pie_chart" title="Ice-time presets">
    A preset divides playing time between your four lines. You do not pick which opponent line
    yours will face.
  </Rule>
</Rules>

## Before the match

<Rules>
  <Rule icon="search" title="Scouting the opponent">
    Once per match. It shows the strength of the opponent's saved lines and a report from up to five
    recent games. The opponent may still change its lineup before kickoff.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="military_tech" title="Preparation bonus">
    Scouting also gives your team a small bonus for that match, which grows with your professional
    scouting staff.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="signal_cellular_alt" title="Match stakes">
    One bar for an ordinary league game, more in a fight for the playoff or a better seed, all five
    in the playoff. Each team can have different stakes. The higher they are, the more mentality
    counts in tense moments.
  </Rule>
</Rules>

## During the match

<Rules>
  <Rule icon="visibility" title="What you see">
    Who is on the ice and each player's energy, goals, penalties and injuries as they happen, and a
    running box score.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="edit" title="What you can change">
    Lineup and tactics. A saved change plays from the moment it takes effect until the end.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="sports" title="Pulling the goalie">
    In the last five minutes of the third, when behind, you can pull the goalie for an extra
    attacker.
  </Rule>
  <Rule icon="movie" title="Highlights">
    Goals and selected chances are shown as animations chosen to match the real event - the manpower
    situation and the number of assists.
  </Rule>
</Rules>
