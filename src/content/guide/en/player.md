## Attributes and ratings

**Technical attributes** - shooting, passing, puck control, decisions, positioning, coverage, stick
checking, body checking, blocking and skating - decide how a player handles play with and without the
puck. **Physical attributes** - speed, strength and stamina - decide who gets to the puck first, who
wins a battle and how fast a player tires. Goalies have their own: reflexes, puck tracking, rebound
control, glove and blocker.

The app summarises them as ratings for attack, defence, physical strength and goalkeeping, plus
experience.

## Player types

Every player has a type. Forwards are snipers, playmakers, power forwards, two-way, defensive
forwards or grinders; defensemen are shutdown, disruptor, puck-moving or offensive; goalies are
butterfly, hybrid or athletic. The type sets how the attributes are
spread: those it is built on sit above the player's level, the rest below. It shifts the balance of
technique and physique, decides which tactics suit the player and which attributes grow faster in
training. The types follow real player profiles used by coaches (see Sources).

## Experience and form

**Experience** grows with every game, fastest for young players, and makes performance steadier.
**Form** is a bonus or penalty of up to ±2 on the attributes, based on how a player has played over
roughly his last 10-20 games compared with what was expected of him. It is not a swing from one game
to the next. An arrow shows it: blue up is best, red down is worst.

## Traits

Each player has five traits that develop slowly or not at all:

- **Talent** - how fast and how far he develops technically,
- **Professionalism** - how he prepares; it shows in physical training, recovery and how long he
  keeps his fitness with age,
- **Injury resistance** - how easily he gets hurt; it drops with age and after serious injuries,
- **Discipline** - how often he takes a penalty and gets drawn into a fight,
- **Mentality** - how he handles pressure in big moments; it can grow from games played under
  pressure.

The club only knows an estimate: two arrows show the lowest and highest possible level, and the real
one lies between them. For your own players the estimate narrows over time with professional
scouting.

The traits are game abstractions. From research the game takes only the direction - for example that
personality becomes more stable with age, or that a previous injury raises the risk of another - not
the numbers (see Sources).

## Energy, fatigue and injuries

- **Energy** drops during a game and comes back on the bench; a player low on energy plays worse.
- **Fatigue** builds up with games and hard training and caps the energy a player starts a game
  with. It goes down with rest - by 5 when the afternoon and the evening begin and by 10 overnight.
- **Injuries** keep a player out of games and training for the blocks the injury lasts; better
  medical staff shorten them. At kickoff the best healthy reserve takes the injured player's place.
  Once per game day an injured player can watch an ad to shorten his recovery by one block.

## Development and age

Players improve through training and games, young ones fastest and more slowly as they approach
their potential. From around thirty the body fades on its own - speed first, stamina from the
mid-thirties - while experience keeps veterans steady until they retire.
